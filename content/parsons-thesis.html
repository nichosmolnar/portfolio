Are we running out of our favorite Japanese food? Sushi is threatened by its own popularity. Once a niche, regionally specific cuisine, modern sushi has become a global product that has pushed its own ingredients to the brink. Sushi’s main player is the bluefin tuna, a fish the sushi industry has transformed from low-grade, low-cost product into one of the most valuable and overexploited meats on the planet.

The Last Sushi in the World is an interactive website that asks a simple question: are we running out of sushi, and if so, when? My thesis work visualizes publicly available data from world fisheries, logistical chains, and technological advancements to answer this question. I use recognizable chart forms and maps to reveal data sequentially to readers. The important context for this data is never far away from the visuals, guiding the reader through a deceptively complex and surprisingly hopeful narrative. Ultimately, this project aims to update the reader’s understanding of sushi, and familiarize themselves with the costs and futures of a cuisine enjoyed by many.

This site was coded in HTML, CSS, JavaScript, using the Vue.js framework. It uses the D3.js library for data visualization and the Mapbox library for mapping. The site is responsive and optimized for desktop and mobile devices.
